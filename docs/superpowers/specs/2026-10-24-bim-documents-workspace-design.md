# BIM documents workspace: design

Date 2026-10-24. Status: draft v2.1 (2026-10-24), after the three-lens review and the check of v2, for the founder's approval. Author: Claude, from the brainstorm with the founder and two research reports:
`docs/research/bim-documents-platforms-2026-10.md` (23 platforms, Sentinel gaps G1 to G14) and
`docs/research/bep-practitioner-pain-points-2026-10.md` (about 30 practitioner voices, the founder's three hard parts).

Marks used below. **Founder:** a decision the founder made, in his words or close to them. **Call:** a choice Claude made; correct it if wrong. Everything else is derived from the research or the code and is cited. A number in brackets such as [F25] names the review finding (security, ISO and builder lenses, 43 findings, all accepted) that caused the change. ## 1. Intent and why

Sentinel's Documents today is a section editor in a narrow side panel: one fixed template per document type, one plain-text box per section, a four-state status, append-only versions, a browser print. It works, and it has one thing no researched platform was shown to have: a clause only scores when it is bound to a check Sentinel runs, the naming section installs as the enforced rule, and every write goes to the hash-chained ledger.

The founder wants Sentinel to create and adopt the ISO 19650 documents (BEP, EIR, TIDP, MIDP and their tables) with the quality of the best AEC platforms, in a workspace of its own. The research says the field is thin: only Plannerly and Morta author these documents in-product; the big CDEs file them as PDFs. The practitioners' worst part is not the writing but writing a plan before the client has said what it wants and before the disciplines who must follow it agree, after which the plan promises unchecked things and goes stale. The founder's own hard parts were describing the office's capabilities, applying ISO 19650 together with the office's standards, and coordinating the BEP with other disciplines.

So the workspace must do three things beyond editing: make the document structured (tables, matrices, live rows), make it shared (section leads, permissions, comments, recorded decisions), and keep it adopted (checks, installs, revision triggers). Because the BEP becomes a contract document and a client decides on it, the review of v1 found that the sign-off must be enforced in the database, not only in the bridge; section 10 now says how.

## 1a. What this builds on

The Documents module shipped in August 2026 as six merged phases (design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-06-bim-documents-module-design.md`, plans beside it): the schema (migration 0020, `bim_documents` and append-only `bim_document_versions`), the templates, the `/bimdocs` bridge routes and `bimdocs-store.mjs`, the Documents tab, print to PDF, EIR and BEP ingestion with propose-then-commit, the check registry with bindings and the compliance report, the MIDP and TIDP deliverable tracker (migration 0022, `/deliverables`), per-section AI drafting with the integrity check, and six read-only MCP document tools. This design keeps all of it and changes four things: the section content (blocks instead of one text body), the surface (a full page instead of the side panel), the people layer (access, section leads, comments, review rounds) and the export. Nothing in the August module is removed except the TIDP prose template, whose job the live view takes over; existing TIDP documents stay readable [F36]. Section 16 lists every old-panel function and where it lives in each release [F38].

## 2. Decisions already made

- **Founder:** one comprehensive BEP document that holds everything, including capability and capacity, mobilisation and the risk register, like the Snowdon pre-appointment BEP. The guidance's preference for a short BEP with linked documents is recorded as a known tension (pain-points report, section 4), not followed.
- **Founder:** in Sentinel the client and all disciplines have access to the document with different permissions (viewer, edit) and the ability to add comments, as Plannerly handles it; then a document approval step at each stage, and updates.
- **Founder:** the client can open the BEP at any time, including while it is being written, as a viewer or commenter.
- **Founder:** layout A, a full-page view inside the Sentinel app, not a separate browser window (mockup `layout.html`, 2026-10-24).
- **Founder, earlier (lifecycle roadmap, decisions 7 and 8):** the three-step review chain (task team check, lead authorisation, client acceptance), and the UK 2021 National Annex status codes.
- **Call, clarifying the bullet above [v2.1]:** the three steps are the BEP's; other document types have shorter chains of their own (section 7).
- **Founder, standing rule:** no JSON or CLI for users; every setting and workflow gets a friendly screen.
- **Founder (2026-10-24, after the builder check):** each project membership carries a party: appointing party, lead appointed party, or appointed party with its task team. A review step names a party as well as a minimum role.
- **Founder (2026-10-24):** as in real projects, the architect's office creates and owns the project, and the client joins as a viewer; the client must still be able to decide the acceptance step. Sentinel changes to match; the Snowdon test seats are swapped to match once the party model ships (the lead's account becomes owner, the client's account a viewer with the appointing party).
- **Founder (2026-10-24):** an invite flow is in scope. Adding a member must not require creating the account in Supabase first.

Two tensions are recorded, not resolved against the founder: the comprehensive BEP against the guidance's short BEP (above), and the comprehensive BEP against its own lifecycle, since mobilisation and risks change weekly while the BEP is under change control. Section 4 resolves the second with living annexes [F22]. The three-step chain stays three steps; section 7 names what each step means at each stage under ISO 19650-2, which is an adaptation of the container chain's labels, not a weakening of it [F13].

## 3. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals (each tied to a release in section 11).**
1. A BEP written as sections of typed blocks: text, table, matrix, facts, live view, proposed changes, EIR link, LOIN, naming rule, project documents (R1a, R1b).
2. An office register, written once, that fills the capability, software, people and term-map blocks of every bid, each line marked evidenced or committed and published into documents only when marked so (R1c) [F7].
3. A lineage from the pre-appointment BEP to the confirmed BEP, with an explicit "Appointment indicated" step between them, a compare view between any two versions, and a label, revision and suitability code on every version (R1b, R2d) [F16, F19].
4. The MIDP and TIDP as live views of the deliverables table, printable as BEP appendices; no second copy (R1c).
5. Section leads from the project members; per-document access as can read, can comment or can edit; comments with resolve, assign and mention; read receipts per version (R2c).
6. Review rounds per stage: who must decide is the roster snapshotted when the round opens, one recorded decision per person, on a BEP the appointing party's step last (an EIR's chain ends with its acceptor, by type), every decision on the ledger (R2d) [F4, F34] [v2.1].
7. A professional export: rendered text and tables, cover page with container ID, revision and code, contents, revision table, appendices (R1b) [F16].
8. Adoption kept and widened: templates shipped pre-bound and scored honestly; new checks for nominated people, one responsible task team per element, and (later) exchanges made as scheduled; LOIN as a table that compiles to IDS (R1b, R3) [F33].
9. Guidance in the screen: a template picker that says when to use what, short guidance per section, a "what to do next" strip on a new document, a one-page summary view (R1b, R3) [F43].
10. Works in the published That Open app and the local app, with the sign-in the user already has, reached in-app from the Documents tab, the Next strip and the Journey (by tab label) and Deliverables. The Revit pane keeps its text hint, as today (R1b) [F26]. **Call:** a document id as a boot parameter, which a launched browser or the add-in could use, is a later item.
11. Members are invited by e-mail from the Members screen, with a role, a party and a task team; the account is created by the bridge when the invitation is sent, and the invitee signs in with the existing code sign-in (R2b) [F35].

**Non-goals for the first release.** Legal e-signature (recorded in-app acceptance is the sign-off; **Call**). Word or Excel export and Word round-trip import (PDF first; **Call**). The information protocol itself, as a template or as legal text: it is an appointment document of the appointing party (5.1.8), not BEP content; the BEP only cites it through the project documents block [F23]. Notification e-mails. Exchange of documents with other CDEs. A separate browser window (an "open in window" button may come later). Deep links into a document from outside the app [F26].

**Anti-goals.** A long template that invites copy-paste (the picker offers minimal and full variants and lets sections be removed). Browser dialogs (the platform sandbox kills them, F-LC-6; use `ask.ts`). JSON or spreadsheets for users. A second source of truth for deliverables, naming, people, risks or mobilisation [F22]. Any change to the append-only versions or the ledger's meaning. Hiding the "declared versus controlling" honesty of the executability score, including by scoring empty sections [F33]. A review step anyone can satisfy by editing a row through PostgREST [F2, F4].

## 4. The content model (**Call**, derived from gaps G1, G2, G5, G13)

**Document.** `bim_documents` keeps `doc_type`, `title`, `status` (wip, shared, published, archived) and `sections`. Added: `stage` (pre-appointment, confirmed, revision), `suitability` (S0, S1 to S5, A1 to An, as the project's information standard allows) [F16], `responds_to` (document id and version number of the EIR it answers, nullable, same project only) [F12], `issuer` for an EIR (the appointing party, or the lead on behalf of a named appointed party for a 5.4.3 EIR, whose `responds_to` may point at the parent EIR) [F17], `template_id` and `template_version`, and `access` overrides (section 6). On insert the new columns are constrained in the database: stage takes the default, suitability is S0, access is empty unless the caller is a lead [F10]. Versions in `bim_document_versions` stay append-only; a version carries `label`, `stage`, `suitability`, a revision as the information standard numbers it, and a `sha` computed by the database (section 10) [F2, F25]. A label may be edited afterwards only through a separate "label note" stored beside the snapshot, never inside it (G7).

**Section.** `id`, `heading`, `guidance`, `owner`, `state`, `bindings`, and `blocks[]`. `owner` stays an e-mail string, as today and as Snowdon's BEP v2 holds it, resolved to a member at read time and shown by name; an e-mail that matches an invited member shows as "invited" until that member is active, and only an e-mail that matches nobody shows as "not a member" [F21] [v2.1]. The UI calls it the **section lead**, never "owner" [F29]. Changing a section lead is a lead's act and is refused while a round is open [F4]. Sections can be added, removed, renamed and reordered through the store with the same locking and audit as `patchSections`; each change writes a ledger row with the old and new outline (G1). Removing a section that carries a binding asks first and its ledger row names the check that left [F33].

**Blocks.** Each block has `id`, `kind`, `title` and a kind-specific `data`. Block data is untrusted on read as well as on write, because contributors can insert document rows directly [F39].

| Kind (UI name) | Holds | Edited as | Exported as |
|---|---|---|---|
| text (Text) | Markdown | a text area with a preview | rendered text |
| table (Table) | typed columns (text, person, team, date, choice, number) and rows; optional row groups by task team | an in-page grid with add and remove row; a lead adds, renames and types columns and sets picklists from installed rulesets [F31] | a table |
| matrix (Responsibility matrix) | rows (elements, deliverables or activities) against task-team columns, optional exchange milestone and recipient columns; marks **produces**, **contributes**, **signs off** [F20, F32] | a grid; the legend says each mark in words; exactly one "produces" per row (the responsible mark, checked) | a table |
| facts (Project facts) | read-only values from the project record, members, offices and the office register | chosen from a list of plain labels ("Appointing party name", "Software we use") with the current value shown beside each [F32] | text or a table |
| live view (Live list) | a stored filter over `deliverables` (MIDP, or TIDP by team), the office register, the risk register or the mobilisation plan | a filter form; rows are edited where they live; the TIDP view renders the existing `/tidp` report with "Edit in Deliverables" as the only edit path [F36] | a table |
| proposed changes (Changes to the client's naming) | the difference between the office naming ruleset and the client's information standard, from `diffNaming`; defaults to "No proposed changes" | review the generated list, add a reason per change | a table |
| eir link (Answers EIR clauses) | the EIR clauses this section answers, with an "unanswered" list computed per document | a clause picker from the linked EIR | a reference line |
| loin (Information needed, LOIN) | rows of element, milestone, property, value, used by and why; rows link to matrix rows by element and milestone [F20] | a grid | a table; compiles to IDS (section 8) |
| naming (Naming rule) | separator, enforce mode, and fields each with label, pattern, allowed values and placeholders, mapped one to one onto `NamingRuleset` [F30] | a form with one row per field; validation in plain words ("Field 3 has no allowed values and no pattern; add one") | a table |
| references (Project documents) | protocol, information standard, production methods, EIR: title, version or hash, issuer, date received, and an "incorporated in appointment of X" marker per party [F23] | a form; the EIR row fills itself from `responds_to` | a table |

**Matrix presets (Call).** Three presets ship [F20]: high-level responsibility matrix (5.3.2 D, rows are information types, columns task teams), detailed responsibility matrix (5.4.2, rows are deliverables or elements with exchange milestone and recipient), and information management assignment (Annex A, rows are ISO activities, columns are the appointing party, the lead and the appointed parties). The check "one responsible task team per element" reads the "produces" mark.

**Migration and the derived body (Call).** An existing section's `body` becomes one text block. From then on `body` is the derived plain-text rendering of all the section's blocks (tables flattened to lines), rewritten by the store on every block change, so the executability "empty" count, AI context, the EIR compile text and `findNamingCandidate` keep working [F28]. The old panel's save replaces the first text block and refuses, with a plain message ("This section has tables; edit it in the workspace"), if the section holds any other block. The mirror is dropped when the old section editor goes, at the end of R3, when the old panel becomes the Documents list (section 16). Migrated sections stay text until converted: "Make this a naming rule" reads a pipe table or a json fence and proposes a naming block for review, propose-then-commit, and `findNamingCandidate` keeps reading body fences as a fallback [F30]. Snowdon's BEP v2 has pipe tables and no json fence, so it migrates as ten text blocks.

**Freezing (Call, from the append-only rule) [F25, F37].** One rule everywhere:
- A draft renders live: facts, live views and register lines read current data, and the cover and every page say "Draft, live data".
- At **Send for review** the document is rendered and frozen: every block, facts, live rows and register lines included. The frozen render and its sha live on the round. Reviewers read and decide on that frozen render. The final accept (or, at tender, the issue) writes exactly that render as the version, byte for byte; withdraw or reject voids it (a tender version already issued at step 2 stays on the record).
- Without a chain (and for READINESS), the freeze happens at publish, after the same preview.
- A published or issued version renders only its frozen snapshot, in the Read view, the PDF and the appendices. "Since then" is a separate button that compares the snapshot with live data, is never printed, and is served only to the roles allowed to read the live source (the delivery roles) [F7]. Compare works on frozen snapshots only. Viewers, the client included, read versions only through the bridge's version route, which applies the render mask below; the direct read of `bim_document_versions` is narrowed to contributor and above (section 10) [F7] [v2.1].

**What a freeze may contain (Call) [F7].** Register lines enter a document only when the line is marked "publish in documents". Facts come from an allow-list of labels that excludes personal data beyond names and roles (no e-mails, no phone numbers). Access overrides and internal fields (bindings history, AI notes, lock data) are stripped from the snapshot. Before send, the sender sees a preview of exactly what will freeze. A line frozen by mistake is hidden by a render mask the bridge applies when serving the version, ledgered with the reason; the append-only row is never touched.

**Bindings stay on the section (Call).** Blocks carry no bindings; `executability.mjs`, the compliance report and the naming install keep reading section bindings. A block may suggest a binding for its section (the existing binding-suggest path), nothing more.

**Office register (Call) [F6, F21].** A new office-level artefact, `office_register`, edited through forms like the other standards (people and information-management functions with competence and evidence, software schedule with versions, hardware and IT, past projects, training plan, term map). Each line carries `evidence` = evidenced (a past project, a certificate, a Sentinel record such as a READINESS measured item) or committed (an action with an owner and a date), and the "publish in documents" flag. It is stored and versioned like the other office artefacts (canonical sha) but in a new **office-only, lead-read class**: never inherited by projects, never listed by the generic artefact route, readable only by members of the office with lead or above. Documents get register lines only through stored block filters rendered by the bridge, never through a filter supplied by the caller. The register belongs to the office attached to the project, the lead firm's office.

**Capability and capacity per task team [F21].** 5.3.3 is each task team assessing its own capability; 5.3.4 is the lead's summary. So the capability table is one row group per task team code. Each group is entered and evidenced by an active member of that team, with the same evidenced or committed marker; an invited member cannot act, so a prospective party's rows are entered after its first sign-in [F21] [v2.1]. The lead's own group fills from the register. The 5.3.4 summary appendix is generated from these rows. A party shows as "prospective" while the document is at the pre-appointment stage; this is derived from the stage, never a column [F21] [v2.1].

**Living annexes (Call) [F22].** The mobilisation plan and the risk register are each one register (one per project, tracked tables beside the deliverables), shown in the BEP as live views and frozen only inside the version snapshot. Their owners tick and edit them without reopening the BEP. The rule for when a change needs a BEP revision: a change to responsibilities, to the information delivery strategy, or to a referenced standard; other changes do not. A change of the first kind raises "needs a review" (section 7).

**Templates (Call) [F24, F31].** Shipped templates stay JSON files on disk for the developer, but each document records `template_id` and `template_version`, and office templates are an office standards artefact edited through a form that covers every field a template has: section headings and guidance, block kinds and order, column definitions and picklist sources, matrix columns and preset, fact labels, live-view filters, pre-bound checks and their parameters, the minimal or full variant, and a name with "when to use" text. No field exists only as JSON. Shipped for the first release: BEP comprehensive (pre-appointment and confirmed stages in one lineage; minimal and full variants), EIR (unchanged shape, now with clause ids), READINESS (unchanged). The pre-appointment BEP offers two starts. **"EIR received"** links the ingested EIR. **"No EIR received"** inserts the Part E p.24 statement that the project is not yet following ISO 19650 and the bidder has anticipated the requirements, plus a clause 5.3.2 checklist. Both starts include an **EIR assumptions and clarifications** table (clause, answered / assumed / excluded / query) fed by the ingested EIR when there is one. A client-supplied BEP template or contents list becomes the skeleton through the existing ingest. The TIDP prose template is retired.

## 5. The workspace (layout A)

A full-width page inside the app, reached from the Documents tab ("Open workspace"), from the Next strip and the Journey (both by tab label, as they work today), and from Deliverables ("Open in BEP") [F26]. **Layout spike first (R1b) [F27]:** a "Workspace" layout (`"workspace" 1fr / 1fr`) with its own header (project name, document title, a Back button), kept off the activity bar, and a check that returning to Projects re-attaches the 3D viewer with its camera and models. **Call:** the layout name is not a setting the founder sees; the only entry points are the buttons above.

- **Left: outline.** Document title, status in plain words, stage, suitability code, version label and what it responds to. One row per section with the section lead's initials and four chips by default: open comments, unbound, empty, needs a review; the rest (committed lines, installed) behind "More" [F43]. Filters: mine, open comments, unbound, changed since version N. Add, remove and reorder sections here (lead and above).
- **Centre: the document.** One section at a time or continuous scroll (builder's choice), each block with its plain kind name, edit in place, "Add block" between blocks. The guidance for the section is one click away. Saving a block keeps the scroll position and open state (G14). Draft with AI works per text block, with the existing cite-a-fact gate; team-only comments never feed a draft of a section the client can read [F1].
- **Right panel, three tabs.** *Quality:* the score as "x % controlling, y of z sections written", the variant beside it, bound checks and their last result, unbound sections, the planned checks named as planned [F33]. *Review:* the current round (section 7), with who must decide and what is frozen. *Comments:* threads for the open section and the document, with resolve, assign and mention, a filter for unresolved, and the audience on every thread.
- **Views:** Edit, Read (rendered as the export will look), Summary (R3), Compare (two versions, section by section), Versions.
- **A new document** opens in Edit with the guidance open and a three-line "what to do next" strip: pick a start (EIR received or not), name section leads, write and then send for review [F43].
- Every question or confirmation uses `ask.ts`.

**Text rendering (Call) [F39].** A small hand-rolled renderer builds DOM nodes from Markdown: headings, lists, bold, pipe tables, and links to http and https only. No raw HTML, no `innerHTML` with document text. Print goes through the existing popup route (`docs-panel.ts` opens a window and prints it), because in-frame `window.print()` is blocked by the sandbox. Snowdon's pipe tables will now render as tables, so "renders unchanged" means content unchanged, now rendered.

## 6. People, permissions and comments

- **Roles.** Project roles stay the base (0004): owner and lead govern (create, structure, send for review, archive); contributors edit any section, as today, and comment everywhere; viewers read and comment. No one loses an edit right they have now. A section lead is accountable for the section, not its only editor. **Founder:** viewers, including the client, may open the document at any time, including a draft.
- **Who owns the project (Call) [F42].** In data, "the architect's office owns the project" means both: the lead's account holds the project role owner, and the project is attached to the lead firm's office, whose owner is that account. The first drill (R1a) makes this true on snowdon-lifecycle: the lead's account becomes the office owner and the lead firm's other people join the office.
- **Per-document overrides [F10].** A lead can set, per member or per task team, can read, can comment or can edit on a document, lowering or raising the project role (a client who is a viewer may be made able to edit one document), and name section leads from the member list. Overrides are stored on the document and enforced by the bridge in one helper, `requireDocAccess(key, doc, level)`, which every section, block, binding, plan, AI, ingest and structure writer calls; a test lists every writer and fails if one calls only the project-role check. Reads cannot go below the project's viewer right by design.
- **Comments [F1, F40].** Threads on a document, a section or a block; fields: author (verified identity), text, created, assignee, mentions, and an audience shown on the thread. **Audience (Call):** everyone who can read the document, or the delivery team. The delivery team is every party except the appointing party. The default is delivery team for members of the lead appointed party and the appointed parties, and everyone for the appointing party. A delivery-team thread is readable by every member of a delivery party, viewers included. Members with no party read and write "everyone" threads only, and their default audience is everyone [v2.1]. Delivery-team threads live in their own store with a store-aware read policy (section 10), so a client cannot read them through PostgREST either; their ledger rows are neutral (no section, assignee or text); their live events go only to the delivery roster's streams. Resolve and assign are appended events folded at read, so both bags stay append-only. Every reader (the panel, the audit pack, MCP tools) uses the same filtered read. Read receipts: the bridge records who opened which version and when, shown as "read by 3 of 4" on the review tab.
- **Notifications.** In-app only in the first release: "My sections", "My reviews", "Mentions" lists on the Dashboard and in the workspace header. Notification e-mails are a non-goal; invitation e-mails are not.
- **Invitations (founder, rules by Call) [F8, F35].** On the Members screen a lead or owner types an e-mail and picks the role, the party and, for an appointed party, the task team.
  - Only on projects attached to an office, by a lead or owner of that project (the rule `addMember` already has, cde-10). Role at or below the inviter's; owner only by an owner; the appointing party only by an owner.
  - The bridge creates the account with the service credential, while public sign-up stays closed, and inserts the membership with the inviter's forwarded JWT, so the database's role cap still applies.
  - One response for new and existing addresses ("Invitation sent"), so the screen is not an e-mail oracle.
  - Per-user and global invite budgets through `takeWriteBudget`, so one user cannot exhaust the shared sign-in mail limit.
  - The e-mail says: open Sentinel, choose "Sign in with a code", enter this e-mail. After the first code sign-in the app offers "Set your password". No link carries a session, because the app never reads a session from its URL (SEC-5) and redirects fight the platform iframe. The mail template change is a builder item.
  - The membership exists at once in an *invited* state, so documents can already name the person as a section lead, shown as "invited" until they are active; it becomes *active* on the first sign-in. An invited member cannot act: they never count as a member for checks, quorums or review steps [F34], and they write nothing, capability rows included; their team's capability rows are entered after the first sign-in [F21] [v2.1].
  - Invitations can be resent and revoked, and expire after 14 days (**Call**), recorded in Sentinel's own invitations record, which is independent of the Supabase code lifetime because the code is requested at sign-in. Revoke and expiry delete the account if it never signed in. Any redirect URL is fixed in configuration, never taken from the request. No login link is ever returned or logged.
  - Ledger rows, reserved names (section 10): member_invited, member_joined, invitation_revoked.
- **Who owns what (founder).** The lead appointed party's office creates and owns the project; its leads govern. The client is a member with role viewer and party appointing party; the client reads, comments, decides the appointing party's steps (EIR issue, tender evaluation, acceptance; section 7), and edits only what a lead grants per document (per-document overrides above) [v2.1].

## 7. Review, acceptance, stages and suitability (**Call**: reuse the container review chain, party-aware)

Sentinel already has a review chain for container versions (migration 0043): ordered steps with a minimum role, one decision per person, the submitter cannot decide, a rejection needs a note and returns the version to work in progress, the last approval publishes. Documents use the same machinery with party-aware steps, per-stage meanings and a frozen render.

**This is an adaptation of ISO 19650-2, named per stage [F13].** The container chain's steps are the delivery clauses (5.6.3/5.6.4 check and approve, 5.7.2 authorise, 5.7.4 accept). A BEP is not delivered under 5.7: it is established by the lead (5.3.2), evaluated at tender (5.2.3, 5.3.7), confirmed after the appointment is indicated (5.4.1) and incorporated in the appointments (5.4.6, 5.4.7). The three steps keep their order and role mechanics; their labels, outcomes and codes change by stage. There is an appointing-party step at every stage, as the founder decided; the table names it.

| Stage | Step 1 (each task team) | Step 2 (lead) | Step 3 (appointing party) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-appointment (tender) | Task-team confirmation (5.3.3) | Lead sign-off of the tender response (5.3.7) **issues** the version with S4 or S5 as the information standard allows; the tender response goes to the client, the round stays open and the lock stays on [v2.1] | **Evaluation:** received / clarification requested / selected / not selected; the decision closes the round [F14] [v2.1] | Submitted (issued with an S code), never Published |
| Confirmed | Each appointed party confirms (5.4.7) | Lead confirms (5.4.1); S5 | **Accepts** (5.4.6) or rejects with a note | Published with an A code |
| Revision | As confirmed, as change control on a contract document | As confirmed | Accepts or rejects | Published with a new A code and revision |

**The tender in steps [F14] [v2.1].** The round opens locked. Step 1 is the task teams' confirmation. Step 2, the lead's sign-off, issues the version with its S code: the tender response goes to the client, the round stays open and the lock stays on. Step 3, the appointing party's evaluation, records received, clarification requested, selected or not selected, and closes the round; "clarification requested" returns the document to wip for a revision. Step 3 cannot be skipped when the roster has a member of the appointing party. So the client acts at every stage, which is what the founder's "approval step at each stage" asks.

**Document states keep their ISO meaning: this is the one rule [F15].** The round opens with the document locked in wip ("Out for review"). It becomes Shared, with the S1 to S3 code the sender chose from the standard (default S3), only when step 1 passes. It carries S5 after step 2; at tender, step 2 issues the version with S4 or S5 and the round stays open for the evaluation. It is Published, with the A code the sender chose at send from the standard's list, only on the final accept. Withdraw and reject void the round and return the document to wip in place with suitability S0 and a ledger row; the next edit is the next wip revision. When step 3 closes a tender round, the document likewise returns to wip with S0, and its issued version shows as Submitted. A document never sits in Shared with no open round [v2.1]. The UI shows the plain words of section 17 with the ISO state and code as a secondary label.

**The chain is scoped by document type [F17].** The scope lives in the `review@n` schema as a steps list per document type, validated (section 10) [v2.1].
- **BEP:** the three steps above.
- **EIR:** two steps, issuer then acceptor. A client's EIR: the appointing party issues it (minimum role viewer, so the client can, while the lead sends it), the lead appointed party acknowledges it. A lead's EIR to an appointed party (5.4.3): the lead issues, that appointed party accepts.
- **READINESS:** no appointing-party step and exempt from the publish gate; a lead publishes it directly, as today [F38].
- TIDP prose documents are retired; the task team's confirmation of its TIDP is its step-1 accept on the BEP whose appendix holds its rows [F18]. Existing TIDP documents stay readable and can be archived; they are never published: `review@n` has no TIDP steps and the transition route refuses publish for a TIDP, chain or no chain [F17] [v2.1].

**Send for review** (lead or owner; for an EIR also a member of the issuer party with edit access). The sender sees the freeze preview (section 4), types a deadline and, for confirmed and revision, picks the A code that the final accept will apply. The bridge then freezes the render and writes the round-start row, which snapshots [F4, F18, F25]:
- the frozen render and its sha;
- **who may decide:** every active member, by uid, with their party, task team and role at that moment;
- **the task teams owed for step 1:** every task team code that has a section (by section lead) or a row in the document's appendices (TIDP and MIDP rows, capability rows, matrix "produces" marks), the lead's own task team included; one accept is owed per task-team code, by an active contributor or above of that team;
- the stage, the chain version and the chosen codes.
`review_decide` reads this roster, never live memberships, so a party change during the round has no effect. Any eligible member of the step's party may decide; there are no separately named reviewers (**Call** [F34]). "My reviews" lists the rounds where the user is eligible.

**Decisions.** One decision per person; the sender cannot decide, as for containers, and so never decides step 2: on a project with no other lead or owner the round cannot proceed, Send for review is refused and the screen says "Invite a second lead" [v2.1]. A member decides only a step that names their party; a step with minimum role viewer is allowed only when it names a party [F11]. Steps 1 and 2: accept, accept with comments, or reject with a note. A rejection voids the round, returns the document to wip with S0, keeps the comments and opens an issue, as container rejections do [v2.1]. Step 3 at confirmed and revision: accept or reject with a note. Step 3 at tender records the evaluation outcome against the issued version and closes the round [v2.1]: "clarification requested" returns the document to wip for a clarification revision, with the client's note as its reason; "not selected" lets the lead archive; "selected" enables "Appointment indicated"; "received" records receipt only. Every decision is a ledger row written by a database function on entity type `review` [F9]. The acceptance record shows, beside each decider, their party, who set it, when, and the ledger row [F4]. **Call [F4] [v2.1]:** there is no stronger rule on who may hold the appointing party. In plain words, the trust is this: the lead firm's owner invites the client; the ledger records who inserted the appointing-party membership and when, and the acceptance record shows the party and the person who decided; a reader can check both.

**Locked while open [F5].** While a round is open, every section writer refuses through one shared lock helper, and a database trigger refuses changes to the document's sections and status except through the round functions; comments stay open. At tender the lock stays on through the evaluation, until step 3 closes the round [v2.1]. "Withdraw from review" (lead or owner) voids the round and its frozen render with a ledger row and returns the document to wip with S0 [v2. Withdraw is allowed at any point before step 3 closes the round. At tender, once step 2 has issued the version, that version stays on the record (versions are append-only) and is shown as "Submitted, evaluation not recorded"; the round and the lock end and the document returns to wip with S0 [v2.1].1]. Nothing decided in a voided round carries over.

**Transitions and the publish gate [F3].** Once a review chain is installed on the project, the transition route loses "published" as a target, and the only moves between wip and shared are Send for review and Withdraw, each writing its round ledger row. A document reaches Issued or Published only through a round, at every stage including the tender (**Founder:** an approval step at each stage). The machine credential is refused for decisions and publishes. Without a chain, a lead or owner publishes directly, as today, with the freeze at publish. A bridge test covers every transition pair. The gate applies to BEP and EIR documents. READINESS publishes directly as today, and on a project with "The client is not in Sentinel" the lead's recorded acceptance is the final step of the round, so Published is still reached only through the round [v2.1].

**A step nobody can decide (Call) [F34, F18] [v2.1].** Skips are narrow. The skip rule applies only to the tender stage and to steps other than step 1:
- **Step 1 at confirmed and revision is never skipped [F18].** A task team owed step 1 with no active member blocks the round: Send for review is refused, naming the team, until the team has an active member or a lead removes that team's sections and appendix rows, each removal ledgered. At tender a lead may skip a step-1 team with no active member, with a reason on the ledger.
- **Step 2 is never skipped.** The sender can never decide it; with no other lead or owner the round cannot proceed (section 7, Decisions).
- **Step 3, the appointing party.** When the roster has a member of the appointing party, it is never skipped, at any stage. When it has none: at confirmed and revision the round cannot be sent at all unless the project setting "The client is not in Sentinel" is on (next paragraph), in which case the lead's recorded acceptance is step 3; at tender a lead may skip it with a reason on the ledger, the round closes, the document returns to wip with S0, and the issued version is shown as "Submitted, evaluation not recorded" [v2.1].

**The client is not in Sentinel (Call) [F34] [v2.1].** A project setting, "The client is not in Sentinel", is set and cleared by an owner, and each change is ledgered; it is refused while the project has an active member of the appointing party. On such a project the appointing party's step is recorded by a lead as `accepted outside Sentinel on <date> by <name>` (at tender, the evaluation outcome with the same date and name), with an optional evidence file, and the document can reach Published. The acceptance record says so in plain words, for example "Accepted outside Sentinel on 3 November 2026 by Jane Client; recorded in Sentinel by Sam Lead". READINESS keeps its exemption from the publish gate.

**Stage changes [F19].** "Appointment indicated: open confirmation" (lead or owner, after a tender outcome of selected or with a note when Sentinel has none) records the date and actor on the ledger, sets the stage to confirmed and opens a new draft revision from the issued tender version. After each appointment is signed, a lead records "Incorporated in appointment": a ledger row naming the document, the version sha and the parties whose appointments include it, shown on the version and in the project documents block. The compare view shows what changed since the tender.

**Revision triggers (G7, pain point 3.4) [F19, F22].** The document shows "needs a review" with the reason until a lead starts a round or dismisses it with a note, when: the project stage changes; a member's party, task team or role changes; a party or task team joins; a standard the document references is reinstalled; the EIR it responds to is republished; or a living-annex change touches responsibilities, strategy or a standard. A late deliverable is not a trigger; it shows in the MIDP view only.

**Sign-off strength (Call):** the recorded decision (name, uid, time, frozen sha, ledger row) is the sign-off. E-signature is out of scope until a client asks for it.

**One chain for both.** The party-aware steps also apply to container versions, so one `review@n` governs both; the change to `review_decide` is tested on containers first (R2a) [F41].

## 10. Storage, ledger and database enforcement

The bridge checks first and the database refuses what the bridge would refuse, because the anon key is public and a member's own JWT reaches PostgREST directly (0033's header). Every rule below gets a probe in the style of `probes/0038_probe.sql` [F2]. Migrations are numbered after the latest (0048 at the time of writing).

- **Documents.** `bim_documents.sections` holds blocks; new columns `stage`, `suitability`, `responds_to`, `issuer`, `template_id`, `template_version`, `access`, constrained on insert (section 4) [F10]. A same-project trigger, as `cde_same_project` in 0043, refuses a `responds_to` or any document or version id stored inside blocks that points at another project; the bridge resolves such references only within the document's project and renders live views only from the stored filter [F12]. A lock trigger refuses section and status changes while a round is open, except through the round functions [F5]. Delete is restricted to the project owner and refused while any version exists [F2].
- **Versions [F2, F25].** Insert on `bim_document_versions` becomes service-only (the authenticated policy is dropped). A BEFORE INSERT trigger rejects any signed-in caller, computes `sha` in the database from the stored jsonb (never supplied by a caller, the canonical-sha lesson of phase 3), requires the snapshot's document id to match the row, requires on every path, chain or no chain, READINESS included, that a canonical sha of the snapshot's sections equals a canonical sha of the document row's current sections [v2.1], and, when a chain applies, requires the sha to equal the frozen sha on the round. New columns: `sha`, `stage`, `suitability`, `revision`, `round_id`. The authenticated read policy is narrowed to contributor and above; viewers, the client included, read versions only through the bridge route, which applies the render mask (section 4) [F7] [v2.1].
- **Memberships [F4, F8, F34].** `party` (appointing, lead_appointed, appointed), `task_team` (a task team code, nullable) and `state` (invited, active). A trigger guards them: they change only by a project owner, never on one's own row, never while a round is open on the project, and the trigger writes the ledger row itself, as `cde_project_chain` does, so a PostgREST edit is recorded too. The same trigger lets only an owner insert a membership with the appointing party. The service credential is exempt from this trigger for exactly two writes, each still ledgered: the state flip from invited to active on first sign-in, and the creator's initial party at project creation (the creator is owner and lead appointed party). So every new project has a party on its creator; any later change to an owner's party needs a different owner or a platform admin [v2.1]. Step eligibility and checks count active members only. Task teams link to members instead of bare e-mails. Inserts are different from changes: a lead or owner may invite a member with a lead-appointed or appointed party and a task team; only an owner may insert an appointing-party membership; after the insert, party, task team and state change only as stated here [v2.1].
- **Invitations [F8].** A table with row-level security on and no policy (service-only), as `platform_admins`: token reference, inviter, expiry, revocation.
- **Review chain [F11, F17].** The `review@n` artefact gains a party per step and holds a steps list for containers and one per document `doc_type` (bep, eir; readiness and tidp have none), validated: an unknown type, an empty list, or a list for readiness or tidp is refused [v2.1]. The validator and `review_decide` require a party on every step and refuse role viewer without one. Once any document on the project has been accepted, a BEP steps list whose last step is not the appointing party is refused; an EIR list ends with its acceptor by type (section 7), and the rule does not apply to it [v2.1].
- **Client outside Sentinel [F34] [v2.1].** A project setting `client_outside_sentinel`, changed only by an owner through a trigger that writes its ledger row and refused while the project has an active appointing-party member. A step recorded outside Sentinel stores the date, the name, the recording lead's uid and an optional evidence file reference. Installing, changing or emptying the chain is the owner's act, ledgered and shown on the round.
- **Rounds [F4, F25].** Either generalise the container review tables to an entity type or add document review tables that mirror them (builder's choice), with the same rules enforced in the database as 0043 does. The round-start row holds the frozen render, its sha (computed in the database), the roster, the task teams owed, the stage, the chain version and the chosen codes.
- **Comments [F1, F40].** Two bridge-only stores: `doc_comments` (audience everyone) and `doc_comments_team` (audience delivery team). A store-aware read policy, "delivery party, any role", lets every member of a delivery party read the team store, viewers included [v2.1]; `bridge_docs_read` excludes it for the appointing party and for members with no party, who read and write the everyone store only. Both bags hold appended events (comment, resolve, assign, mention) folded at read. Read receipts go in a small new table.
- **Office register [F6].** A standards artefact in the new office-only, lead-read class of `artefact-store.mjs`, with versions and a canonical sha, absent from `KINDS` listings and the generic route.
- **Ledger [F9, F1].** All writes go through the bridge with the verified identity as actor; identities inside `new_value` (decided by, assignee, resolved by, party) come from `currentSub()` on the server, never from the body, and each row records the uid beside the e-mail. Decision rows are written by database functions on entity type `review` (already reserved). New action names are added to `RESERVED_ACTIONS` so the machine credential cannot write them: member_invited, member_joined, invitation_revoked, membership_party_changed, document_structure_changed, document_stage_changed, document_incorporated, review_step_skipped, review_step_recorded_outside, client_outside_sentinel_changed [v2.1], review_chain_changed, version_masked, and the comment audience rows. Ledger rows for team-only threads use their own entity type with a neutral action name and no section, assignee or text.
- **Machine credential [F3].** Refused for review decisions and for any publish or issue of a BEP or EIR once a chain exists, as 0043 refuses it for containers; READINESS and the container paths are unchanged [v2.1].
